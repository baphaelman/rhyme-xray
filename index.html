      Multisyllabic rhymes

        How Rhyme X-ray decides what rhymes

        Sounds, not spelling

        Each word is looked up in the CMU Pronouncing Dictionary. Lyric spellings are normalised first ("spinnin'" is read as "spinning" with the g dropped, "tha" as "the", "'em" as /əm/, numbers as words). Anything still unknown is guessed from its spelling and gets a dotted underline. Sounds are shown in IPA.

        The rhyme part of a syllable

        A syllable's rhyme is its vowel plus every consonant up to the next vowel, so "squeal-ers" can rhyme with "wheels". The tiers follow Pat Pattison's songwriting taxonomy:

        • Perfect: same vowel and same consonants (/is/ grease, peace).
        • Near: same vowel; consonants from the same family (stops, fricatives, nasals; voicing ignored) or one extra consonant (wheel / wheels, grease / threes).
        • Assonance: same vowel only (sit / spin). Rap analysts treat this as the backbone of modern rhyme. It is drawn lighter.

        Who gets coloured

        Stressed syllables of content words, the last syllable of each line, and full unstressed endings like the -y in "sweaty". Function words ("the", "a", "on") are coloured only as part of a longer multisyllabic run.

        Multisyllabic rhymes

        Two runs of syllables match in order, starting on a stressed syllable. Stressed positions must match at the chosen tier. Unstressed schwas may stand in for each other. One unstressed syllable in the middle may break the pattern, because performers swallow those. Each run is underlined in one colour so it reads as a unit.

        Where sources disagree

        Rap analysts agree that the vowel carries the rhyme. Malmi et al.'s Raplyzer measures nothing else. They disagree on consonants and on repetition. Hirjee & Brown and the WSJ's Hamilton analysis score the consonants after the vowel. Wadsworth & Needle count an identical word as an "identical rhyme"; Raplyzer explicitly scores it as zero. That is why "a word rhymes with itself" is a switch.
